# Deus Ex: UNATCO Route - Indicative Full-Campaign Concept

*A possible 3–4 mission continuation for a JC Denton who remains loyal to UNATCO.*

*Working design document, not a final script.*

> **6 October 2026 revision —** The New York / Hong Kong rewrite below is a design to implement, not a description of the installed build. The complete new dialogue is in [the rewrite script](CAMPAIGN_REWRITE_2026_10_06.md). Later missions remain indicative.

> **About this document.** This is the campaign treatment the mod is being developed from: where the
> story could go after what is already playable. It was written before and during implementation, so
> some details (and some sample lines) differ from the game. For what the mod actually does, see the
> [narrative design document](NARRATIVE_DESIGN.md) and the [status](STATUS.md). Sections 5 to 8 are
> the part still to be built.

> **IMPORTANT —** This document is deliberately indicative. It is a campaign treatment, not a locked screenplay or a claim about the exact cut storyline Ion Storm intended. Mission order, characters, maps, dialogue and endings can be changed after implementation tests and further research.

## 1. Campaign Premise

The campaign begins at the unused/alternate branch in Mission 04: JC investigates Paul’s claims, does not send the NSF distress signal, and refuses to defect from UNATCO. From that point onward, the campaign asks a different question from the retail game:

**If UNATCO is compromised, do you destroy it — or try to save it from inside?**

JC does not become an “evil route” version of himself. He remains procedural, skeptical and loyal to the idea of UNATCO. Paul believes the institution is irredeemable; JC initially believes corruption can be isolated and corrected. The dramatic arc is the slow destruction of that certainty.

### Design pillars

- Reuse original Deus Ex maps, goals, alliance systems and conversation flags wherever possible.
- Preserve player agency: investigation order, optional conversations, stealth/combat and non-essential NPCs should remain flexible.
- Do not reveal the conspiracy all at once. Every mission should provide evidence that can still be rationalized until late in the campaign.
- Keep JC recognizable. He does not suddenly become cruel; he continues to distinguish evidence from ideology.
- Gunther, Anna, Simons, Tong, Maggie and Jock should keep their original speaking rhythms and motivations.
- InfoLinks should normally be one-way mission communications. Major arguments happen face-to-face.
- The campaign should feel like a plausible lost branch of the original game, not a separate total conversion.

## 2. Proposed Campaign Structure

| **Block** | **Location** | **Core Objective** | **Narrative Function** | **Status** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branch / Prologue | Hell’s Kitchen → Battery Park | Reject Paul, remain UNATCO, resume Hong Kong assignment | Locks the alternate timeline; Gunther/Anna react to previous choices | Already largely designed |
| Mission 1 | Hong Kong | Locate Tracer Tong and his network | JC sees MJ12 from the inside and becomes the instrument used to find Tong | Current detailed mission |
| Mission 2 | VersaLife, Hong Kong | Officially investigate Tong’s stolen/compromised data | JC discovers the Gray Death/VersaLife link while still acting under UNATCO authority | Next mission |
| Mission 3 | New York / PRCS Wall Cloud | Protect a strategic installation and suppress NSF sabotage | Reverse a retail mission; JC returns home with serious doubts and meets the institutional consequences | Indicative |
| Mission 4 | Vandenberg / X-51 | Participate in the assault on X-51; confront Paul and MJ12 priorities | Forces JC to define loyalty: institution, command, or principle | Indicative |
| Optional Finale | Area 51 / UNATCO power struggle | Choose between Page, Simons, or reclaiming UNATCO | Provides endings that do not collapse back into the retail route | Highly indicative |

## 3. Prologue / Branch — “Separate Ways”

> **Indicative scope —** This is not counted as a full new mission. It is the repaired end of Mission 04 that allows the new campaign to begin.

JC has already been ordered by Manderley to travel to Hong Kong and take out Tracer Tong. Jock diverts the flight to New York so JC can meet Paul. JC investigates the NSF facility but refuses to send the signal. The existing alternate Paul conversation is used as the point of divergence.

### Sequence

1. JC tells Paul that UNATCO is imperfect but he will not become a terrorist.
2. Paul accepts that the brothers are taking separate paths.
3. Paul remains in the ’Ton while JC is inside. The instant JC exits, Paul disappears offscreen and remains alive.
4. Gunther arrives with two Special Agents. He is in a hurry to capture Paul and briefly challenges JC for having left a wanted man free.
5. If Anna is alive, she can appear at the subway checkpoint. Her dialogue varies according to the 747 outcome and the information she has received.
6. JC reaches Jock at Battery Park. Paul is not coming. JC orders Jock to resume the original flight to Hong Kong.

### Key Gunther beat

Gunther reproaches JC and resumes the arrest operation. JC does not win the argument.

**GUNTHER:** Denton. You should be on your way to Hong Kong. Did you see Paul?

**JC:** I spoke to him.

**GUNTHER:** And you left him here.

**JC:** He tried to recruit me. I refused.

**GUNTHER:** You should have arrested him. Manderley will hear about this. Get out of the way.

As Gunther passes JC, in first person:

**JC:** Bring him in alive.

**GUNTHER:** You had your chance.

The optional interior sequence is a search, not a lecture. One voluntary interaction gives the reproach about letting Paul escape; another tells JC to put Paul's evidence in his report and follow orders. Further interactions close with “Go to Hong Kong, Denton.” The shortened script and the Anna-death opening are in the [6 October rewrite](CAMPAIGN_REWRITE_2026_10_06.md).

### 747 continuity variants

- Anna alive / JC killed Lebedev: Anna can contrast JC’s decisiveness with Lebedev against his failure to arrest Paul.
- Anna alive / Anna killed Lebedev: Anna criticizes JC for repeatedly leaving others to finish his assignments.
- Anna alive / ambiguous Lebedev outcome: use neutral dialogue; never invent who killed Lebedev.
- Anna dead: no Anna at the subway. A generic UNATCO trooper may guard the route; Gunther becomes more suspicious but does not automatically know JC killed Anna.
- Knowledge must be separate from facts: a character may be dead while Gunther lacks proof of who killed them.

## 4. Mission 1 — Hong Kong: “The Hunt”

This revision makes JC the investigator conducting the operation. The locations and loyalist route remain familiar; faction bargaining and the physical access granted to Special Projects now determine how the mission develops. Full dialogue: [6 October script](CAMPAIGN_REWRITE_2026_10_06.md).

### Act I — Authorized arrival

The helibase welcomes an active UNATCO agent. Preserve Jock, the officer and the variants for hearing Majestic 12 mentioned earlier. A readable field authorization identifies Special Projects and its local issuing office.

Simons's one-way InfoLink makes JC responsible for the investigation:

**SIMONS:** Denton. Special Projects has developed several leads on Tong's network. You will coordinate the local investigation.

**SIMONS:** Start with the Red Arrow. We believe Tong is using the conflict between the Triads to conceal his movements.

**SIMONS:** Locate him and report from a secure terminal. Special Projects will handle the transfer once you have him contained.

Initial goals: **LOCATE TRACER TONG** and **ESTABLISH CONTACT WITH RED ARROW**. Maggie becomes an investigative lead rather than the mandatory first stop.

### Act II — Competing offers

An optional messenger and the existing Red Arrow escort lead to Max Chen. Max offers an introduction to Gordon in exchange for the Dragon's Tooth and evidence about Yuen Kong. He wants to strengthen the Red Arrow, not merely help UNATCO.

Maggie claims the Luminous Path stole the same weapon and offers reconciliation if JC recovers it. JC can challenge that account using Max's claims only if he has heard them.

Gordon refuses access until JC brings something verifiable. Paul earned his trust; a UNATCO badge does not.

The investigation distinguishes the sword's location, the transcript involving Yuen and the police death report. A weapon found in Maggie's apartment proves deception about its location, not murder by itself. The Simons/Maggie recording proves coordination, not the Gray Death conspiracy.

### Act III — Custody and access

Three approaches can reach Tong:

- Deliver the sword and the collected evidence to Max. Red Arrow gains the weapon and negotiates a truce.
- Bring evidence directly to Gordon. He can arrange a guarded meeting and a local ceasefire without pretending Max received the sword.
- Retain the sword as evidence, or leave it in the case while documenting it. Access comes through the records; Max remains dissatisfied.

The weapon transfer is a real inventory transaction. Its ownership and the extent of the truce remain visible in later reactions.

Gordon permits a meeting because Tong wants to examine the records and confront JC. He does not consent to Tong's arrest, become a friend of UNATCO or erase the reason JC came.

**GORDON:** You expose Chow and expect us to forget why you're here.

**JC:** I'm here for Tong.

**GORDON:** Exactly.

### Act IV — Comparable evidence

Tong offers documents instead of demanding allegiance. The shipment IDs already used in the mod are compared with Paul's separately readable copy. The helibase field authorization is compared with a VersaLife Level Two annex from Special Projects.

These are leads with provenance, not a complete revelation. A hostile source may have altered them; JC wants the originals at VersaLife. Lines vary when he has not read Paul's copy or the helibase record.

**JC:** Your records say Simons has access to VersaLife. They don't prove UNATCO is involved.

**TONG:** I didn't say they did.

**JC:** I want the originals.

**TONG:** Then keep looking.

The manifest can be read and copied without triggering the raid.

### Act V — JC opens the access

The player explicitly reports Tong's location and requests a transfer team at the secure terminal. Simons instructs him to open the northern maintenance access. The squad waits outside.

The current map has no verified northern assault access: the door, console and staging vestibule must be built on a copy. Do not rename the southern entrance or reuse Tong's escape route.

JC uses credentials or hacking and activates **OPEN NORTH MAINTENANCE ACCESS**. The lock clicks, the physical door opens, and Special Projects enters through that passage. Only then do the alarm, armed resistance and sweep begin.

**TONG:** They didn't send you to kill me.

**TONG:** They sent you to open the door.

**TONG:** Now watch what comes through it.

The ending of a conversation, a timer, Tong's death or a map transition must not bypass the opening action. If JC leaves before opening the access, the objective stays pending. Offscreen resolution is allowed only after the operation has started.

### Mission ending

Tong uses a separate escape route. Special Projects copies selected archives, destroys other records and detains personnel. JC can save data or stop the execution of an unarmed technician; that objection alone does not convert the campaign to the retail route.

Simons authorizes the next investigation at VersaLife. The report describes what JC actually recovered or witnessed, with variants for Tong dead, an uncopied manifest and events resolved offscreen.

**End-state:** JC remains an active UNATCO agent. He has concrete records to verify and has personally enabled an operation whose orders differ from his own. The wider Gray Death proof belongs to the next mission.

## 5. Mission 2 — VersaLife: “Authorized Access”

> **Indicative —** This mission is less locked than Mission 1. Its purpose is to exploit retail VersaLife from the opposite perspective: JC enters through the front door as an authorized investigator.

Target length: 60–90 minutes. Mission 2 should begin quietly. The horror comes from the fact that the institution welcomes JC rather than fighting him.

### Act I — Front door

VersaLife security recognizes JC. Staff may already have retail-friendly lines such as identifying him with Simons’ office. This should be leveraged instead of replaced wherever possible.

**RECEPTIONIST:** Agent Denton? We were told to expect someone from Mr. Simons’ office.

**JC:** I’m reviewing a security breach connected to Tracer Tong.

**RECEPTIONIST:** Security will make the relevant records available.

### Official objective

- Determine which systems Tong/Luminous Path penetrated.
- Recover or identify compromised nanotechnology research.
- Verify the Ambrosia shipping records recovered from Tong.
- Do not begin the mission with an explicit objective to expose VersaLife or MJ12.

### Act II — Legitimate investigation

The public and upper laboratory areas are accessible. JC interviews staff, reads internal mail and compares shipment identifiers. At first, every anomaly has a plausible explanation: restricted medical research, anti-terror security, classified government contracts.

The player should gradually discover that the records Tong provided are genuine. Some staff know only pieces. One or two researchers may genuinely believe they are working on treatment or containment rather than weapon production.

### Act III — Level 2

The investigation eventually reaches an area that JC’s official clearance does not cover. The player must decide whether to stop and report, obtain/steal higher clearance, or enter covertly. This is a major role-playing beat: JC can remain loyal while knowingly exceeding his authorization because the evidence requires verification.

> **Design principle —** Breaking a clearance restriction is not the same as defecting from UNATCO. The campaign should allow JC to believe he is protecting the organization by investigating what its leadership refuses to explain.

### Core discovery

- The Gray Death and Ambrosia are part of the same controlled research chain.
- VersaLife laboratories are producing or maintaining material directly connected to the epidemic.
- MJ12/Special Projects has access to the restricted program.
- UNATCO logistics have been used to protect or move material connected to the program.
- The evidence implicates leadership above Manderley, but does not necessarily prove that ordinary UNATCO personnel know anything.

### The Simons moment

The most important beat should not be a villain confession. JC files or uploads enough data that Simons clearly knows what he has found. Simons orders him to stop digging and return for debriefing, framing the research as a classified containment program that JC is not cleared to understand.

**SIMONS:** Denton. Your report has crossed into material outside your clearance. Stop the search and return to the helibase. The files you recovered will be reviewed through the proper channels.

JC does not answer. The objective changes to Return for debriefing. The player may still explore before leaving.

### Possible ending variants

- Compliant: JC leaves immediately with the evidence already logged.
- Investigative: JC searches one additional restricted area and recovers stronger proof.
- Destructive/violent: JC attacks friendly personnel or sabotages the facility; this creates discipline flags and makes later trust much harder, but should not instantly force the retail fugitive route unless the player commits openly hostile acts.

> **Mission 2 end-state —** JC can no longer rationally believe the problem is merely Maggie or a rogue field operation. He still distinguishes “UNATCO” from the people controlling it, which becomes the central conflict of Mission 3.

## 6. Mission 3 — New York: “The Wall Cloud”

> **Indicative —** This mission uses the community idea of reversing the PRCS Wall Cloud objective, but the exact map order and target can change depending on technical feasibility.

JC returns to New York for debriefing while still officially serving UNATCO. He now knows that VersaLife and Special Projects are involved in the Gray Death program, but he does not know how far that knowledge extends through UNATCO.

### Act I — Return to the institution

- UNATCO personnel remain friendly. This is important: the headquarters must feel like JC’s home, not an enemy dungeon.
- Alex is uneasy and may privately confirm that records have been removed or altered since JC’s report.
- Sam Carter represents the ideal of what UNATCO claims to be.
- Manderley may be defensive, frightened or partially ignorant depending on the direction chosen later.
- Gunther is still hunting Paul and may be frustrated by continued failures.
- If Anna is alive, her attitude reflects the player’s earlier performance; if dead, her absence remains a persistent consequence.

### Official mission

The NSF or Paul’s network is preparing to attack/sabotage the PRCS Wall Cloud. In the retail game JC works against the system; here he receives the opposite order: secure the installation and prevent the transmission infrastructure from being destroyed or compromised.

> **Why this works —** The objective gives JC a legitimate reason to continue serving UNATCO even after VersaLife: protecting communications infrastructure from a terrorist attack is still defensible. The moral problem comes from what the system is being used to protect.

### Mission play

- Defend or retake sections of the facility instead of sabotaging them.
- Capture NSF operators when possible; lethal force remains optional.
- Intercept communications showing that Paul’s side is trying to expose information, not merely destroy infrastructure.
- Find evidence that Special Projects is using the same network to suppress or redirect data related to the epidemic.
- Allow JC to complete the official objective while secretly preserving evidence that Simons wanted destroyed.

### Paul pressure

Paul should not necessarily appear physically yet. A datacube, intercepted message or third-party report can establish that he is still active and knows JC has returned. Avoid turning every mission into a direct brother confrontation.

### End beat

After JC protects the Wall Cloud, the institution congratulates him, but the evidence he preserved now demonstrates that Special Projects is manipulating UNATCO infrastructure. The contradiction becomes impossible to ignore: JC has completed the mission successfully and simultaneously proven that the chain of command is compromised.

> **Possible transition —** Simons responds not by arresting JC immediately, but by assigning him to an operation where loyalty can be tested under controlled conditions: Vandenberg/X-51.

## 7. Mission 4 — Vandenberg: “Chain of Command”

> **Indicative —** This is the moral climax of the core campaign. It should not simply be “vanilla Vandenberg but enemies reversed.” The player must be placed between legitimate security concerns and unmistakable evidence of MJ12 control.

UNATCO/Special Projects launches an assault on X-51. Officially, Savage’s group has stolen strategic technology and is collaborating with terrorists. From the perspective of a loyal UNATCO agent, this accusation is not absurd.

### Act I — Assault

- JC participates in the operation instead of defending X-51.
- Gunther can fight on the same side as JC, creating a striking inversion of the retail campaign.
- MJ12 commandos and regular UNATCO personnel operate together, but their priorities diverge once sensitive technology is reached.
- X-51 personnel may surrender; how UNATCO and MJ12 treat them becomes another test of what “security” means.

### Act II — Paul

If Paul is alive, this is the strongest place for the real brother confrontation. Paul is no longer a sick man in a hotel asking JC to trust him. He has survived, gathered evidence and chosen a side. JC has also had time to discover the conspiracy for himself.

**PAUL:** You’ve seen it now. VersaLife. Special Projects. How much more do you need?

**JC:** Enough to know you were right about some things. Not enough to make the NSF right about everything.

**PAUL:** You still think you can save UNATCO.

**JC:** I think people like Simons don’t get to decide what it is.

These lines are indicative, not final. The important point is that JC does not simply say “you were right.” Their disagreement has evolved: it is now about method and ownership of institutions, not facts.

### Act III — the order that breaks the chain

The decisive event should be an order that JC can no longer interpret as legitimate security. Examples include executing surrendered X-51 scientists, destroying evidence solely to protect Page, or killing Paul after he has offered to surrender. The exact trigger should be chosen based on gameplay implementation.

### Core branch

| **Choice** | **Meaning** | **Result** |
|---|---|---|
| Obey | Loyalty means loyalty to the current chain of command | JC remains with Simons/MJ12 and moves toward the dark loyalist ending. |
| Refuse the specific order | Loyalty is to UNATCO’s mandate, not to illegal commands | JC breaks with the people controlling UNATCO without joining the NSF. |
| Join Paul/resistance outright | JC finally accepts Paul’s method | Optional convergence toward a more familiar anti-MJ12 route; should not be the only “good” outcome. |

> **Recommended core ending —** For this project, the most distinctive route is the middle one: JC refuses MJ12 while explicitly claiming he is defending UNATCO from capture by Page/Simons. This preserves the premise instead of turning the campaign back into vanilla.

## 8. Optional Finale — Area 51 / “Who Owns UNATCO?”

> **Highly indicative —** This can be omitted if scope requires only three or four new missions. Mission 4 can carry a satisfactory ending on its own. If included, Area 51 should be shorter and focused on resolution rather than another full investigation.

By the finale, the struggle is no longer UNATCO versus terrorists. It is a conflict over who controls the infrastructure, personnel and legitimacy of global security.

### Possible factions

- Bob Page: wants direct control and eventual Helios integration; regards UNATCO as a tool.
- Walton Simons: may oppose Page if Page’s ambition threatens the existing power structure; Simons can represent authoritarian continuity rather than liberation.
- JC: can choose to serve one of them, destroy both, or attempt to reclaim UNATCO’s command structure.
- Paul/Tong/Illuminati-aligned forces: may offer intelligence or tactical support without necessarily becoming JC’s faction.

### Ending A — Loyalist / Simons

JC concludes that stability matters more than the origin of the conspiracy. He helps Simons remove Page or consolidate control. UNATCO survives but becomes openly authoritarian. JC is rewarded with authority inside the organization.

**SIMONS:** Your brother could never separate the institution from the people running it.

**JC:** Maybe he understood them better than I did.

**SIMONS:** And yet you’re still here.

### Ending B — Reclaim UNATCO

JC rejects Page and Simons but does not join the NSF. He exposes or removes the leadership structures using UNATCO personnel who still believe in the organization’s mandate. Alex, Carter and other surviving personnel can become important here.

**PAUL:** You finally figured it out.

**JC:** You were right about who controlled it.

**PAUL:** And UNATCO?

**JC:** They don’t get to keep it.

### Ending C — Break with UNATCO

JC concludes that the institution cannot be separated from its architecture of control. This is the route that most closely converges with the philosophical position of the retail campaign, but it should be reached after a much longer internal struggle.

### Ending D — Page / dark route

Optional and intentionally bleak: JC accepts Page’s argument that the existing institutions are obsolete and helps him seize the final system. This can function as the “true evil” branch if the project wants one, rather than treating ordinary UNATCO loyalty as inherently evil.

## 9. Character Arcs Across the Campaign

**JC Denton:** Starts as a loyal agent who sees Paul’s defection as an unjustified leap. Ends by deciding whether loyalty means obedience, institutional reform, or rejection of the institution itself.

**Paul Denton:** Begins as the brother JC refuses to follow. Should remain morally serious rather than smug. His later return is more powerful if he does not constantly contact JC.

**Gunther Hermann:** A parallel loyalist. He demonstrates what loyalty without JC’s skepticism can become. Depending on the ending, he can remain ally, rival or tragic opponent.

**Anna Navarre:** If alive, she represents aggressive mission-first loyalty and continues judging JC’s decisiveness. If dead, her absence materially changes Gunther and removes her later functions.

**Walton Simons:** Should remain plausible for as long as possible. He rarely lies when compartmentalization, classification and procedure will do the job more effectively.

**Manderley:** Can be written as partially compromised, frightened, or genuinely ignorant of the highest-level program. Avoid making every UNATCO leader equally informed.

**Alex Jacobson:** The internal conscience and information broker. His ability to hide logs and notice altered records makes him central to an “inside reform” route.

**Sam Carter:** Embodies the idealized UNATCO that JC believes may still be worth saving.

**Tracer Tong:** Does not need JC to trust him. He needs JC to verify evidence independently. This makes him a philosophical counterpoint to Paul.

**Maggie Chow:** An intelligence asset whose lies reveal how Special Projects manipulates intermediaries. She should remain non-essential to progression after her initial role.

**Jock:** Knows more than JC realizes. He helps Paul but still transports JC, creating tension without forcing an early confrontation.

## 10. Implementation Rules for Claude Code

- Never assume a single 747 outcome. Anna’s state, Lebedev’s outcome and what other characters know must be tracked separately.
- Facts and NPC knowledge are separate state. “JC killed Anna” does not imply “Gunther knows JC killed Anna.”
- Paul disappears from the ’Ton only after JC exits; returning immediately must not respawn him.
- Optional conversations must never carry the only copy of progression-critical information.
- InfoLinks should be one-way by default and must be safe across map transitions; critical flags are set before playback, with recovery/replay logic if necessary.
- Reuse vanilla flags, alliance changes, keypads, truce switches and map geometry whenever technically safe.
- Never let an unknown state silently default to the most common narrative outcome. Use neutral dialogue or explicit test initialization.
- Keep Tong/Max/Gordon invincibility or essential behavior where the retail game already relies on it unless there is a strong reason to branch.
- Maggie can die without breaking progression; essential evidence must remain accessible elsewhere.
- A single objection to MJ12 behavior should not automatically make JC a fugitive. Track disciplinary tension separately from faction hostility.
- If the player openly attacks allied UNATCO/MJ12 personnel on a large scale, the loyalist route may legitimately break or branch.
- NPCs should not repeat exposition that the player already received unless the repetition is character-motivated.

## 11. Scope Recommendation

For a realistic AI-assisted mod, the recommended scope is four major blocks plus the repaired Mission 04 branch. The optional Area 51 finale should be added only after the first four blocks are playable and stable.

1. Phase 1: finish and test M04 branch (Paul → Gunther/Anna variants → Jock).
2. Phase 2: finish Hong Kong through the Tong assault.
3. Phase 3: build VersaLife as an authorized investigation.
4. Phase 4: build one strong New York reversal mission and Vandenberg climax.
5. Phase 5: only then decide whether Area 51 needs a playable finale or whether Vandenberg can carry the ending.

## 12. Final Story Principle

The campaign should never ask, “What if JC became the bad guy?” That is the least interesting version of the premise. It should ask whether a principled person can remain loyal to an institution after discovering that the institution is being used by people whose goals contradict its stated purpose.

**Paul leaves the system because he no longer believes it can be repaired.  
JC stays long enough to find out whether he is right.**

> **Status —** Indicative campaign treatment. Mission 1 has the strongest current design. Missions 2–4 and the finale should be iterated one at a time after playtesting the preceding block.
